Should You Wait for Black Friday to Buy a Hard Drive or SSD in 2026?

Published October 22, 2026 • 10 min read • UK retailer prices and PriceSpy UK ranges checked 22 October 2026

Short answer: for a hard drive, no. For an SSD, only if you already know exactly which drive you want and the price you are willing to pay. Black Friday is on 27 November this year, and storage is still getting more expensive on the way there.

In a normal year, waiting five weeks for a sale on a portable SSD was easy money. 2026 is not a normal year. The AI data centre build-out has pushed hard drive and SSD prices to record highs, and the analysts who track memory contracts expect another rise this quarter. The surprising part of our research is that the timing matters less than the shop. The same drive is currently swinging by 40% or more between retailers and between weeks, which is a bigger gap than almost any Black Friday discount will close.

What Black Friday Normally Does to Prices

Even before the storage crisis, Black Friday was a weaker deal than it looked. The consumer group Which? tracked 175 home and tech products at eight major UK retailers, including Amazon, Argos, Currys and John Lewis, for six months either side of Black Friday 2024. In its November 2025 report, none of those products hit their lowest price of the year on Black Friday itself. 83% were the same price or cheaper at least once outside the four-week sales period.

When genuine reductions do happen, PriceSpy puts the typical discount at 20 to 25%. Keep that number in mind. It is the most a Black Friday deal usually saves you, and as you will see below, it is smaller than the price swings storage buyers are dealing with every week this year.

Why 2026 Is Different for Storage

In most years, Black Friday storage deals ride a falling market. Retailers discount drives that are getting cheaper to make anyway. This year the market is moving the other way.

At the end of September, TrendForce published its fourth-quarter forecast, which covers the whole Black Friday period. It expects NAND flash contract prices to rise 15 to 20% on the previous quarter, driven by AI servers. The detail underneath is more interesting for shoppers. TrendForce says increases for consumer SSDs are more restrained than for data centre drives, that retail channels are struggling to absorb record chip prices, and that consumer SSD buyers are leaning on stock built up in the first half of the year.

Put plainly: shops are sitting on drives they bought months ago at lower cost, and every replacement they order will cost them more. That cuts both ways. Some retailers will be happy to clear older stock with a real discount. Others will hold prices, because they know the next batch is more expensive.

Recent sales back this up. When a price tracker measured matched storage models across Amazon US during June's Prime Day, its index stayed within about 1% of flat, and the share of drives at their own recorded low was no different from an ordinary week. Last year was worse. Two months before Black Friday 2025, SanDisk announced a 10% price increase on its NAND products, and prices kept climbing through the sale and into this year.

Do not expect 2024 prices. Industry tracking published in August showed SSDs averaging 126% above their September 2025 prices, with hard drives 129% higher. A 25% Black Friday discount on a drive that has doubled still leaves you paying far more than you would have two years ago.

Hard Drives on Black Friday: Don't Wait

Hard drives and SSDs run on different clocks, and that shows up clearly at sale time.

Small portable hard drives, the 2TB to 5TB models most people use for backups and archives, have been remarkably stable since the summer. With prices that flat, there is not much room for a meaningful Black Friday cut, and the few pounds you might save are not worth leaving a backup incomplete for another five weeks.

Big desktop drives are a different story with the same ending. Seagate and Western Digital sell almost all their output to data centres on long-term contracts, which is why retail prices have climbed steadily rather than swinging around. "Sale" pricing on these is modest. Scan currently has the Seagate Expansion Desktop 16TB at £505.99, down from £529.99. That is less than 5% off, or about £31.62 per terabyte. If you see a big archive drive meaningfully below that on Black Friday, it is worth a look. Otherwise, buy when you need it.

SSDs on Black Friday: Real Deals, If You Are Ready

Portable SSDs are where genuine Black Friday deals are most likely, precisely because their prices are so volatile. Two things to look for, and one to avoid.

Look for: outgoing models

Retailers holding stock bought earlier in the year have room to discount it, especially where a newer model is arriving. Sandisk is in the middle of replacing its portable range with a new generation of Extreme and Extreme PRO drives, and Samsung's T7 family is several years old. Those are the drives most likely to see real cuts.

Look for: the capacity you actually need

Sales push people up a size. If a 2TB drive covers your next year of work, a "deal" on a 4TB drive at double the price is not a saving. This is where knowing exactly what is already on your drives pays off.

Avoid: brand-new launches

New models are rarely discounted in their first weeks. Scan lists the new 4TB SanDisk Extreme PRO Portable SSD at £1,177.49 on pre-order, due on 26 October, a month before Black Friday. If you want the latest drive, Black Friday is unlikely to help. If you want value, last year's model is where the deal will be.

How to Tell If a Black Friday Storage Deal Is Real

Five steps, and the first three are worth doing this week, before any sale starts.

  1. Work out what you need. Check the free space and duplicates on the drives you already own, then decide the capacity and type you need: a cheap hard drive for backups, or a fast SSD for active projects.
  2. Shortlist exact models. Two or three specific drives, not "a 2TB SSD". Black Friday pages are designed to make every drive look like a bargain.
  3. Write down today's price and its price per terabyte. Then check its three-month history on PriceSpy, or camelcamelcamel for Amazon. A "was" price is only meaningful if the drive was actually sold at it.
  4. Set a target price. If a shortlisted drive drops below it, buy. If it does not, you have lost nothing by not buying.
  5. Check the seller, not just the price. Buy from the retailer itself rather than a marketplace seller, and favour shops with a price promise. Which? notes that AO and John Lewis refund the difference for seven days if the price drops.

Beware of fake drives. High prices bring counterfeits. If a listing offers a portable SSD at a capacity or price that no major brand sells, such as a "16TB portable SSD" for £50, it is fake. These drives report a capacity they do not have and silently corrupt anything written past the real limit. After buying any drive, copy a large folder onto it and check that every file opens. So, Should You Wait?

It depends on why you need the storage. Here is how we would think about it.

You need storage for work in the next month

Buy now

A possible 20% saving in five weeks is not worth a missed deadline or a camera card you cannot clear. Buy what the job needs from the cheapest reputable retailer today. Our 4TB drive roundup is a good starting point.

Your backups are incomplete

Buy now

Small hard drives have been flat for months and are unlikely to drop much. A second copy of your work matters far more than a few pounds. Our budget external drive guide lists five reliable options.

You want a portable SSD and can wait

Wait, with a target

This is the one case where Black Friday can pay. Shortlist two or three drives, record today's prices, and set a target. If the sales do not hit it, there is no rush: SSDs are the category forecast to ease first, from the second half of 2027.

You are hoping for a crash back to 2024 prices

It won't happen

Contract prices are still rising this quarter. Black Friday can give you a better price than today, but not a return to normal. That is a 2027 story at the earliest, as we explain in our 2026 to 2028 price outlook.

Frequently Asked Questions

Will SSD prices drop on Black Friday 2026?

Some individual SSDs will see genuine discounts, especially older models that retailers bought earlier in the year. There will not be a broad drop. TrendForce expects NAND flash contract prices to rise 15 to 20 percent in the fourth quarter of 2026, so the underlying market is still moving up through the sale.

When is Black Friday 2026 in the UK?

Black Friday is on Friday 27 November 2026 and Cyber Monday is on 30 November. Many UK retailers start their sales about a week earlier, around 20 November.

Should I wait for Black Friday to buy an external hard drive?

Usually not. Small portable hard drives have been stable for months, so there is little room for a meaningful discount, and large hard drives are priced by long-term data centre contracts. If you need a drive for work or backups, buy it now from the cheapest reputable retailer.

Are Black Friday storage deals real?

Some are, but many are not. Which? found that none of 175 products it tracked were at their lowest price of the year on Black Friday 2024. Check a drive's three-month price history before buying, and compare retailers on the same day, because storage prices in 2026 often vary by 40 percent or more between shops.

Is it better to buy an SSD now or wait until 2027?

If you need it now, buy now. If you can wait, SSDs are the category forecast to get cheaper first, with TrendForce expecting the NAND flash market to move back into balance in the second half of 2027. A Black Friday deal on a drive you have already shortlisted is a reasonable middle ground.

The Bottom Line

Black Friday 2026 lands in a storage market that is still rising, so it will not deliver the across-the-board drops of previous years. For hard drives, waiting buys you very little: buy what you need, when you need it. For portable SSDs, the sales can pay off, but only for people who arrive with a shortlist and a target price.

Whatever you decide, the shop you buy from will matter more than the day you buy it. And the drive you never have to buy will always be the best deal. Start by finding out what is already on your desk.
